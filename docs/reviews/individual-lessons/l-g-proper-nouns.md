## Teaching decision

Nine short editing tasks start from an actual mis-capitalized draft. Students repair a name,
city, named attraction, weekday, month, language, ordinary noun, first-person I and country.
Three tasks use an original travel illustration with a live caption marked as the learner's own
writing. The picture remains the same while Great Wall changes to the ordinary description
“a long wall.” May and modal may appear together, so capitalization follows meaning.

Written cards provide all identities, locations and dates. Faces and the decorative map are
never treated as evidence of nationality or geographical accuracy. Exact words are code.
The original and phone delivery were inspected. A smaller existing courtyard delivery was also
inspected, preserving recognizable activities while keeping the existing package budget.

Case-sensitive checking rejects mia/MIA/mIa, preserves the actual attempted caption, and allows
the learner to clear the seeded draft without the old typo reappearing. A cleared draft survives
reload. The source caption template remains immutable. Browser input disables auto-capitalization and spelling correction. Three redundant classification steps were removed after inspection.
The visible correction label now describes editing an existing draft. Short typed answers keep
their final punctuation attached; a real wrong May caption exposed this wrapping problem.
