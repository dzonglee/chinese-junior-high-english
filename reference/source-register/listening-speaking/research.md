## What the evidence establishes

The five Fujian provincial original examination books (2022–2026; R-LS::012–016) consistently specify three listening sections: five image-choice items from sentences heard twice, ten multiple-choice items from seven dialogues heard twice, and five one-word table blanks from a stimulus heard three times. All 20 items carry 1.5 marks, for 30 marks. Section subtotals of 7.5/15/7.5 are arithmetic, not separately printed claims. The 2023 third-section instruction calls its stimulus **对话**; the other four years call it **短文**. The record preserves that wording rather than inferring what an unavailable recording contains.

`listening-task-specs.json` cites the exact printed pages. No recording, transcript, pause schedule, total listening duration or speaking rubric was obtained. These are provincial-paper models, with `cities: []`: hosting on Fuzhou or Quanzhou education websites does not establish any priority city's use of the paper.

Each retained Fujian PDF contains the official cover and three whole listening-question pages. R-SOUTH acquired the original books in this session; R-LS independently recalculated each complete parent hash and byte count, visually opened every retained page, and made a four-page extract with PyMuPDF `insert_pdf`. The manifest preserves original URL, parent hash, parent page numbers and R-SOUTH source ID. No duplicate original download or independent corroboration is claimed. Boundary content from adjacent sections remains on the unchanged pages. These are question-page extracts, not complete English papers or answer collections.

Qingdao West Coast's 2026 official notice (R-LS::005) and its linked tables establish 120 English marks including 30 for oral/listening; the combined oral/listening machine examination is 30 minutes, scheduled April 18–19. The written examination is 100 minutes on June 15, 09:00–10:40. The notice describes municipal organization but is published for the district. It does not supply item/task counts, preparation or response windows, scoring dimensions or a named vendor. Only that district-policy scope is verified.

Yantai's 2026 scheme (R-LS::006) covers 2022-entry 初四 students in the 五四 structure: 120 English marks including 30 for oral/listening, human-machine dialogue on April 24. Page display date May 9 differs from metadata June 25; neither is the test date. The 2025 reform PDF (R-LS::007) starts with autumn-2023 entrants, normally graduating in 2027 under 五四, and specifies 90 written plus 30 oral/listening in 初四 lower term. Its municipal question-setting/district organization is explicit. The 2026 machine mode is **not** carried into 2027 without an annual notice. Neither source supplies a detailed oral rubric or task model.

Liaoning's 2021 original scheme, FAQ and cohort reply (R-LS::001–003) establish the provincial framework of 90 written plus 30 listening/oral from autumn-2021 grade-7 entrants, with municipal implementation. The cohort reply specifies grade-9 lower term; the general scheme is broader about upper/lower term. These are framework facts, not verified annual Shenyang/Dalian arrangements. The older letter (R-LS::004) mentions a historical 50-mark machine test, but its city/cohort scope is unresolved and its page/body dates conflict. That 50 must not replace the later 30-mark framework. A previously researched Chaoyang exception further demonstrates why provincial arithmetic cannot substitute for an annual city notice.

Guangdong's original 2018 implementation measure (R-LS::008) distinguishes written plus listening **or** listening/speaking. For province-set papers it specifies 120 marks including 30 listening marks, a 90-minute whole-English sitting and approximately 20 minutes listening. Its explicit five-year validity from August 2018 prevents treating it as a current 2024–2026 rule without an extension. Annual 2023–2025 schedules (R-LS::009–011) separately verify 08:30–10:00 whole-English sittings for cities using province-set papers; they do not establish a 90-minute listening/oral test or name all participating cities. Current replacement policy and city speaking arrangements remain unresolved.
