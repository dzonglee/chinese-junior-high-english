Have you ever wondered why we say maidongxi instead of mainanbei, shangcesuo instead of
xiacesuo, and yijianxiangzuo instead of yijianxiangyou Many Chinese words have directions in
them, and there is lots of history and culture hidden behind.
East or West
When going shopping, Chinese people say maidongxi instead of mainanbei. A theory says that in
the Tang Dynasty, there were two popular markets in Chang'an, the national capital at that time.
One was called the East Market, and the other was called the West Market. When shopping, people
usually went to the East Market first, and then went to the West Market. As time went by, people
started to describe shopping as maidongxi.
Up or down
We use shangcesuo to mean "go to the bathroom" and xiachufang to mean "go to the kitchen".
Why In ancient times, houses were laid out(布局) in a certain way. The gate of the house would
face south with the bathroom in the northeastern part of the house and the kitchen in the
southeastern part. On maps, north is usually at the top while south is at the bottom. In Chinese, we
talk about the north as shang and the south as xia. So people would say they're going up to the
bathroom and down to the kitchen.
Ancient Chinese not only used shang and xia to refer to directions but also social positions. For
example, people used huangshang to refer to the emperor and dianxia to refer to princess who had
a lower position than the emperor.
Left or right
Most people today are right-handed. They feel uncomfortable if they try to use their left hand to write
or use chopsticks. This was true in old times as well. So in ancient China, "left" means something
different, such as in yijianxiangzuo.
When shaking hands, it's important to know which hand to use. In most cultures, the right hand is
generally used for a handshake. For example, in some countries, such as India and Nepal, the left
hand is thought to be unclean. However, if you are in a culture where the left hand is used, you'd
better do as the locals do.

For centuries, southwest China has had a serious transport problem: rich resources and goods
were far from the sea. They had to take longer and more expensive roads to reach world markets.
On September 16, 2026, Pinglu Canal officially opened. It creates a direct waterway to the sea,
connecting southwest China with the Beibu Gulf.
In Guangxi, the 134.2-kilometer canal is a key project. It can carry 5,000-ton ships. By connecting
the Xij River with Qinzhou Port, it makes the old shipping path through Guangzhou Port over 560
kilometers.
People expect it to save about 5.2 billion yuan every year. It will greatly help the trade of goods
such as minerals, coal, and building materials.
Unlike projects built only for money, the Pinglu Canal values both construction and environmental
protection. To protect wildlife, workers moved over 9,570 coastal trees and planted 275,000 young
ones. They also built special paths for fish and a 240-meter bridge for wild animals to cross. These
help restore the travel paths for animals. Over 98% of the dug-out earth is reused.
Besides environmental benefits, the canal boosts local trade. As part of a land-sea trade route, it
helps trade between China and ASEAN(东南亚国家联盟). It allows Chinese goods to reach
Southeast Asia faster and ASEAN resources to enter China easily. Experts believe cargo(货物) will
increase, reaching 150 million tons a year in the future.
Like the Panama and Suez Canals, which changed global shipping, the Pinglu Canal has special
local value. Unlike those canals connecting different oceans, it serves river-and-sea transport in one
single area. Its full value will depend on working together with current ports, railways, and transport
networks.
