“We need to help fix it,” Ollie said, his eyes bright with excitement.

Weeks later, more students began to use the compost box and waste less food, showing that small actions could make a real difference.

For many years, pandas have been China’s most famous animal ambassadors (大使). Now another Chinese animal is beginning to appear in zoos around the world: the golden snub-nosed monkey (金丝猴).
Golden snub-nosed monkeys live mainly in mountain forests in China. They are easy to recognize because of their golden-orange fur (皮毛), blue faces and small upturned noses. To survive cold winters, they grow thick fur. They eat fruit, flowers, seeds and tree bark, and they often live in groups.
These monkeys also have a long history in Chinese culture. They have appeared in paintings, stories and local traditions for many years. However, they are now endangered. Only about 8,000 to 15,000 golden snub-nosed monkeys are left in the wild. The main problems they face are losing places to live, people cutting down forests and illegal hunting (非法捕猎).
China protects the monkeys carefully. Because they come from China, they cannot be sent to other countries without the government’s agreement. This is similar to the way China has protected and shared pandas with other countries.
Recently, a few golden snub-nosed monkeys were sent to zoos in Europe. Some went to France, and others went to Belgium. They will stay there for ten years. Their new homes include trees, ropes and climbing areas, so the monkeys can move in more natural ways.
Supporters say the program can help people in other countries learn about the monkeys and care more about protecting them. It may also support scientific research and international cooperation. However, some scientists worry that long journeys and new environments may bring stress to the animals. They believe the monkeys’ health should always come first.
It is still unknown whether golden snub-nosed monkeys will become as famous as pandas. But their journey shows that animal protection is not only about one country. It can also be a way for people around the world to learn, work together and protect rare animals.

Screens are part of daily life. We use them to study, talk with friends, watch videos and play games. Screens can be fun and useful, but spending too much time on them may make us miss out on other important things.
Health experts suggest that children and teenagers should not have more than two hours of screen time a day. This gives them time to read, exercise, talk face to face and sleep. The key is not to stop using screens completely, but to use them in a smart way.
Why is screen time so tempting Psychologist Jonathan Haidt says many apps and games are designed to hold people’s attention. Bright colours, notices and rewards can make users want to keep watching or playing. When something feels exciting, the brain may want more of it. That is why ten minutes of short videos can easily become an hour.
However, not all screen time is bad. Some screen time can be helpful. For example, if you are chatting with family, looking up information for homework or learning something new, screens can support your life. The problem comes when screens take over your time and stop you from doing things that are good for your body and mind.
Emily, a 12-year-old girl, has made rules for using screens. She sometimes reads instead of watching screens. She also likes going for walks, because she can enjoy the scenery and get exercise at the same time. Her example shows that screen time can be balanced with real-life activities.
To make good choices, you can set a timer before using a screen. You can also keep your phone away while doing homework or before going to bed. Most importantly, use technology as a tool. Do not let it use you. When you control your screen time, you will have more time for learning, friendships and the world around you.
