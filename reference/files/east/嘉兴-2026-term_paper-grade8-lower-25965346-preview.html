I was born with an eye disease called Leber’s Congenital Amaurosis (莱伯先天性黑蒙性视网膜病变), so my eyesight is very poor. I can only feel light and tell if something is in front of me. It also makes my eyes very easily hurt by sunlight. Whenever I go outside, I have to wear very dark sunglasses.
Since I was little, my biggest challenge has been trying to fit in. In school, I had a hard time playing games or doing group activities with the other students. Because of my poor eyesight, I was often left behind.
Things finally began to look up in Grade 3. That year, I was introduced to my first piece of assistive (辅助的) technology. It’s a screen reader called JAWS. When a website is designed to work with it, JAWS can “see” everything on the webpage and read it aloud for me. That made a big difference.
Later, I got a second one—VoiceOver. Thanks to it, I’ve been able to explore more parts of the digital world. I can text and email friends and family. I can also listen to my favorite songs and shop online. Without the screen reader, all of that would have been out of reach.
I have got a lot from assistive technology. However, there is still a lot to improve. Many websites and apps do not work well with screen readers. I hope more developers will care about the needs of users like me. The digital world should be for everyone.

On Jeju Island (济州岛), there lives a special group of women called Haenyeo, or sea women. These brave swimmers dive (潜水) into the sea to collect seafood and sell it at markets.
Haenyeo start training at a young age, often when they are about 10 or 11 years old. They learn to dive deep, many of them can go down 30 meters and hold their breath for 2 minutes at a time. However, deep water puts great pressure on their bodies and causes pain. Sometimes there are also dangerous animals like sharks. In winter, the cold makes their work even harder. Before diving suits were used, these sea women wore cloth suits. These were not good for keeping warm.
Though the work is hard, most of these women are not young. In fact, almost all of them are now over 50 years old. The oldest is in her 80s. She has been diving for more than 60 years! For a long time, Haenyeo have made most of the money for their families. Their work helps pay for housing, food and education (教育). At that time, many women stayed at home, but these women worked outside and made a living from the sea.
In 2016, Culture of Jeju Haenyeo was added to the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists (世界非物质文化遗产名录). Actually, across the sea in China, from Hainan to Fujian, the sea women have also dived into the deep blue, sharing the same spirit and a similar way of life. When you see them, you see years of hard work, pain and courage. They never give up and become a symbol of women’s power.

Many people may think planning is boring, but it’s one of the most useful skills we should learn.
What’s the true meaning of planning
As the saying goes, “If you fail to plan, you are planning to fail.” It tells us that planning is the first step to success. Planning is not just a waste of time. It is creating a timeline and roadmap to reach our goals.
What’s the importance of planning
In fact, we all plan in some way and enjoy its good points in daily life, even without realizing it. For example, planning holidays helps us remember tasks and places before leaving, leading to a better trip. By making a spending plan, we can check our money often. Also, a good plan helps us use time wisely and avoid mistakes in study or work.
How can we start planning
To get started, write down your goals. They can be about study, life or personal hobbies. Big goals often seem difficult, but small steps are much easier to finish. And then start planning how to make them come true. Little by little, your plans will turn big dreams into real achievements!
